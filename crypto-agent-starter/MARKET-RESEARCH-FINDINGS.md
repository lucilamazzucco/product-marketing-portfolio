# Revolut Crypto — UK Market & Competitive Research

**Research date:** 24 October 2026  
**Project:** Independent Product Marketing portfolio research  
**Business question:** How can Revolut Crypto help crypto-curious UK customers understand their options and make informed decisions?

## Executive summary

The initial automated research collected official-source material from Revolut, PayPal, bunq and N26. The workflow produced **45 topic-tagged evidence records**, which are extraction leads rather than 45 independently verified findings. Coinbase was added as a specialist-exchange benchmark but its four candidate pages could not be fetched. The report therefore does **not** establish a comprehensive UK competitor ranking.

**Three research findings from the available official-source excerpts:**

1. **Integrated-app positioning is explicit.** Revolut's crypto page describes access to crypto in the same app used for everyday money management. bunq's European crypto page likewise emphasizes investing without another login. This suggests a positioning theme worth testing, not proof of customer preference.
2. **Fee comprehension is a meaningful comparison dimension.** Revolut's public copy refers to exchange fees that depend on plan and trading volume; its terms also refer to minimum or percentage-based fees and potentially other applicable charges. PayPal says the transaction fee and exchange rate are displayed before confirmation. bunq says its pre-trade price includes the fee and spread. These are **different disclosure descriptions**, not a like-for-like fee comparison.
3. **Customer education and product boundaries matter.** Revolut promotes Crypto Learn and publicly describes watchlists and price alerts. PayPal's UK help page describes limitations on sending or transferring crypto outside PayPal. N26's help page states availability in specific European countries, so N26 is not presented as a direct UK alternative.

## Positioning interpretation — integrated finance as a category proposition

Revolut's public crypto messaging explicitly places crypto in the same app customers use for everyday money management. bunq similarly emphasizes crypto access without an additional login. These observations support an **integrated-finance messaging pattern**; they do not establish which provider customers prefer.

| Positioning dimension | Potential customer value | Tension or research question |
|---|---|---|
| Convenience | Access without starting with an unfamiliar standalone destination | Does this materially influence provider choice? |
| Familiarity | Existing interface and brand relationship | Does familiarity help comprehension or create a false sense of safety? |
| Transparency | Clear explanations of fees, product limitations and risks | Can customers accurately describe the true costs and constraints? |
| Education | Information presented at the point of decision | Does contextual learning support better informed choices? |

**Strategic interpretation:** convenience is a category-level promise, not automatically a unique advantage. Revolut could explore a more specific territory: **familiar access paired with transparent, risk-balanced decision support**. That proposition must be tested with UK customers and assessed for regulatory compliance.

**Proposed positioning direction (not approved copy):** “Explore crypto within the finance app you already use, with clear information about costs, limitations and risks.”

A separate specialist-exchange benchmark is excluded from this initial evidence-backed comparison because the available snapshots were insufficient. The failure details remain in the technical source-coverage report, not the executive comparison.

## Proposed customer decision journey

| Stage | Question to validate | Potential PMM response |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery | What is crypto, and is it appropriate for me? | Risk-balanced introductory education |
| Consideration | Why use a finance app rather than a specialist exchange? | Transparent positioning and limitations |
| Evaluation | What will I pay, and what could I lose? | Plain-language fee and risk explanations |
| Onboarding | What checks and restrictions apply? | Clear expectation-setting before account steps |
| Ongoing use | Where can I get reliable answers? | Accessible help and continuing education |

This journey is a **research model**, not a measured Revolut funnel.

## Strategic recommendation

**Prioritize a comprehension study**, not an acquisition campaign.

Test a proposed plain-language information layer alongside full required disclosures, using moderated sessions with UK-based crypto-curious adults. Compare accurate understanding of costs, eligibility, product limitations and risk against current public explanations. Track clarity and task completion as secondary measures. Reject or revise the concept if risk understanding deteriorates. A compliance review would be necessary before implementation.

**Why this first?** The competitive research shows multiple approaches to explaining integrated access, fees and product boundaries. It does **not** establish whether Revolut's customers struggle with them. A comprehension study directly tests the missing customer insight.

## Limitations and next research

This is a **completed initial desk-research phase**, not completed primary market research. It does not include interviews, a representative customer survey, share-of-voice data, win/loss research, observed customer conversion, or validated brand perception. No claim is made about market share or which product is best. The next phases are a manually verified Coinbase benchmark and primary UK customer research.
