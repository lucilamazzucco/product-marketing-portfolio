## 1. Positioning and messaging
For each competitor: category, primary promise, target user, headline language, supporting proof, CTA. Cite public website, ads, social posts or decks and capture date.

## 2. Target audience and unmet needs
Explicitly stated segments vs inferred segments; use-case and customer jobs; evidence of underserved needs. Mark inferred segments as hypotheses.

## 3. Product and meaningful differentiation
Compare 3–5 customer-valued capabilities, not exhaustive feature lists. Record eligibility, geography, limitations and source links.

## 4. Pricing and packaging
Pricing model, plans, transaction costs, spread, limits, promotions and eligibility. Distinguish current verified prices from unknown or changing fees.

## 5. Go-to-market
Observable owned content, public campaigns, partnerships, referral offers, SEO landing pages, paid ads. Do not infer ad spend, acquisition volume or conversion without data.

## 6. Customer feedback and weaknesses
Dated, sampled reviews and community comments; theme, evidence, sample size, selection bias. Internal win/loss notes only when genuinely available.

## 7. Market movement
Dated announcements, launches, hiring, pricing and regulatory changes. Verify changes before calling them new.

## 8. Brand perception
Dated press/social/review themes. Share of voice requires defined platforms, query, timeframe and denominator.

## Methodology and limitations
Official pages and public statements are first-party positioning evidence, not independent validation. User reviews can be biased; geographic availability differs. Distinguish snapshots, fresh checks, confirmed changes, hypotheses and unavailable information.
