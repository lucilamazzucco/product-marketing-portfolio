# Revolut Crypto — Current Experience Audit & Crypto Discovery Journey

**Independent portfolio concept | 24 October 2026**

## Evidence and scope
This audit is based on four user-provided screenshots of a Spanish-language Revolut Crypto experience displaying CHF prices. The screenshots show: (1) Crypto home with market prices and a Revolut X banner; (2) Learn with crypto courses, suggested Monad content and rewards; (3) Strategies with round-ups, recurring buys, take profit/stop loss and collections; and (4) Earn/staking with listed APYs. They are **not** a complete product walkthrough, UK eligibility verification or proof that particular screens appear in a specific order. The user does not recall the original onboarding and reports seeing a lengthy message; its precise content is not independently verified here.

## Current experience: observation → question → opportunity

| Surface | Direct observation | Unanswered research question | Hypothesis to test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Crypto home | Market prices and performance indicators are prominent; Revolut X banner visible | What do newcomers understand about the differences between crypto features and Revolut X? | A choice-based discovery entry point could improve orientation |
| Learn | Crypto courses and reward-related content are available | Can customers find relevant learning when they need it? | Contextual links from feature pages to relevant learning could improve comprehension |
| Strategies | Round-ups, recurring buys, take profit/stop loss and collections appear together | Can novices distinguish automation, trading controls and product risks? | Plain-language descriptions and user-controlled exploration paths could help |
| Earn / staking | APY figures and assets are prominent | Do users understand variable returns, restrictions and risks? | Contextual risk-and-terms explanations could improve informed understanding |

**Important:** These are questions, not demonstrated UX defects. A user-visible screenshot cannot establish discoverability, conversion or misunderstanding rates. Do not assume Swiss/CHF experience equals UK product availability.

## Strategic shift
**Previous concept:** add more crypto education.  
**Refined concept:** connect **existing** product education and discovery across the crypto journey.

**PMM opportunity hypothesis:** Revolut already offers a broad set of crypto-related features and educational resources. The opportunity may be to help eligible crypto-curious users navigate them through a coherent, contextual, user-directed discovery experience.

## Proposed Crypto Discovery Journey
1. **Entry — “What would you like to understand?”** A neutral discovery module with options such as “How crypto works,” “Costs and risks,” and “Explore available features.” No default trading prompt.
2. **Learn in context.** Link relevant existing Learn content when users explore a feature. Make mandatory disclosures and eligibility information accessible independently of education.
3. **Understand the choice.** For each relevant feature, explain purpose, conditions, potential costs and material risks before any optional action; do not present APY or reward as guaranteed returns.
4. **Self-directed next step.** Customers choose whether to continue exploring, save information or leave. Learning completion does not automatically trigger a trading solicitation.
5. **Optional lifecycle support.** Eligibility-filtered, preference-based educational reminders with opt-outs, frequency caps and compliance review.

## Segment hypotheses
- **Crypto-curious beginners:** need orientation and vocabulary; initial research priority.
- **Occasional users:** may need cost, feature and risk comparisons.
- **Experienced users:** different needs; do not collapse Revolut Crypto and Revolut X into one experience.

Segment membership must not be inferred solely from holding an account, visiting a screen or completing a course.

## Lifecycle concept: illustrative, not deployed
| Moment | Eligibility / trigger hypothesis | Message role | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| First exploration | Eligible customer opts into education | Introduce neutral learning choices | No pressure to trade |
| Feature exploration | Customer requests more information about a feature | Surface relevant existing Learn content and terms | Risks and limitations remain visible |
| Incomplete learning | Explicit educational preference and appropriate consent | Offer to resume learning | Frequency cap, opt-out; no implied investment readiness |
| Post-learning | Customer chooses next step | Offer feature explanation or exit | Never treat quiz completion as financial suitability |

## Research and measurement
**Research 1 — Journey mapping:** moderated tasks with eligible UK adults of different experience levels. Ask participants to find an explanation of fees, risks, and a selected feature. Record success, confusion and navigation paths.

**Research 2 — Contextual education comprehension:** compare existing experience with a prototype linking feature exploration to relevant learning and complete disclosures. Primary measure: correct understanding of feature purpose, material risks, costs and restrictions. Guardrail: no increased false confidence.

**Research 3 — Messaging territories:** compare convenience-only, education-led and integrated-context positioning for clarity, credibility and distinctiveness.

**Potential later business measures:** qualified educational engagement and voluntary feature exploration, with complaint/opt-out and misunderstanding guardrails. No baseline or lift is available; no conversion results are claimed.

## Cross-functional delivery
PMM: segment, positioning, message architecture and prioritization. Product/Design: journey prototype and feature surfaces. Research/Data: validation and measurement. CRM: preference-based educational flows. Legal/Compliance: UK eligibility, financial promotion, risk disclosures and approvals.

## 90-day proposal
- **Days 1–30:** map complete current UK journey, verify product eligibility and disclosures, interview customers.
- **Days 31–60:** prototype contextual discovery, test comprehension and positioning.
- **Days 61–90:** decide whether to pilot approved experience, define analytics and lifecycle safeguards.

## What remains unknown
Actual onboarding screens, exact text of the long message, UK-specific product availability, existing personalization logic, Learn-to-feature analytics, conversion, and customer pain-point prevalence. These require further evidence before calling them product gaps.
