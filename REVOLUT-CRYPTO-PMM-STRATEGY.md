# Revolut Crypto — Positioning & Growth Strategy

**Independent PMM portfolio proposal · 24 October 2026**  
**Status:** first-round official-source desk research completed; customer segmentation, messaging, GTM and experiments below are proposed, not tested. Not affiliated with Revolut.

## Executive recommendation
**Business question:** How might Revolut increase *informed* crypto adoption among eligible existing UK customers without weakening risk understanding?

**Proposed strategic territory:** *Crypto in context* — explore crypto in a familiar finance app with clear, accessible information about costs, limitations and risks. This is a testable direction, not an approved claim or a proven differentiator.

**Why:** Initial desk research found integrated-app messaging from Revolut and bunq, alongside fee, education and restriction-related disclosures across relevant providers. Convenience alone appears insufficient as a defensible positioning hypothesis. Whether users value clarity and familiarity is not established by desk research.

## 1. Segmentation hypotheses and prioritization
| Segment | Working definition | Potential need to validate | PMM approach | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Crypto-curious existing Revolut customers | Eligible adults who use Revolut but have little or no crypto experience | Understand the offer, fees and risks before deciding | Plain-language explanations and optional education | **Primary test audience** |
| Occasional crypto users | Adults who have explored or used crypto but are not frequent traders | Compare cost, product limitations and convenience | Transparent comparison and contextual FAQs | Secondary |
| Experienced traders | Adults familiar with specialist platforms and advanced tools | Evaluate control, functionality and pricing | Separate research stream; do not imply Revolut Crypto and Revolut X are interchangeable | Out of initial scope |

**Selection rationale:** The crypto-curious segment is a useful starting hypothesis because the integrated-app proposition and comprehension questions can be tested without presuming customers want to trade. Segment size, value and behavioral prevalence are unknown; validate with customer and product data before investment.

## 2. Positioning territories
| Territory | Core promise | Strategic strength | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Crypto, simplified | Explore crypto inside an app you already use | Familiarity and convenience | May be easy to imitate; simplicity must not obscure risk |
| B. Understand before you decide | Clear explanations of choices, costs and risks | Focus on informed decisions | Education is not exclusive to Revolut |
| **C. Crypto in context** | Familiar access paired with transparent, risk-balanced information | Connects integration and responsible clarity | Requires evidence of relevance and compliant copy |

**Recommendation:** Test C against A and B, not assume C wins. Proposed copy concept (not approved): “Explore crypto in the finance app you already use, with clear information about costs, limitations and risks.” Avoid safety, returns or guaranteed-outcome implications.

## 3. Messaging architecture
**Audience:** eligible UK adults who are crypto-curious and already use Revolut.

**Value proposition hypothesis:** A familiar place to explore crypto, supported by accessible information that helps customers decide whether it is right for them.

**Pillar 1 — Familiar access:** explain where the experience lives and how to find it; do not claim it is risk-free or inherently safer.

**Pillar 2 — Understand the full cost:** make fees, spread and any relevant conditions easy to locate; verify the actual current terms before publishing specific amounts.

**Pillar 3 — Decide with context:** present risks, eligibility and product limitations alongside educational content, preserving mandatory disclosures.

**Illustrative message by journey stage**
- Discover: “Curious about crypto? Start by understanding how it works and what risks are involved.”
- Evaluate: “See the costs, risks and limitations before making a decision.”
- Optional onboarding: “Review the information and decide whether crypto is right for you.”
These are exploration concepts, not production-ready financial promotions. Compliance review is required.

## 4. GTM activation concept
**Proposed initiative:** *Understand Crypto Before You Decide* — an education-led experience for eligible UK existing customers, not a promotion encouraging trades.

**Channels and responsibilities**
| Workstream | Example output | Collaboration |
|---|---|---|
| Product & UX | Prototype of a concise information layer with links to full disclosures | Product, Design, Legal/Compliance |
| Lifecycle CRM | Optional, eligibility-filtered email/in-app educational sequence; frequency caps and opt-outs | CRM, Data, Compliance |
| Content & Education | Consistent definitions, fee explanations, risk/eligibility FAQs | Content, Product, Compliance |
| Analytics | Comprehension study and instrumentation plan | Research, Data, Product |

**Lifecycle example:** discovery content → optional educational module → comprehension check → user-directed exploration. Do not send transactional prompts solely because a user engaged with risk education.

## 5. Experimentation and success metrics
**Experiment 1 — Comprehension (first):** moderated UK adult research comparing current public explanations with a plain-language prototype. Primary: correct comprehension of risks, costs and eligibility. Guardrail: no deterioration in risk understanding.

**Experiment 2 — Message territories:** qualitative preference and understanding test across A/B/C. Evaluate relevance, distinctiveness, credibility and potential misconceptions; do not use stated preference as proof of conversion.

**Experiment 3 — Controlled activation (conditional):** only after evidence and compliance review, test an optional education-led lifecycle experience among eligible customers. Measure qualified educational engagement, understanding and voluntary continuation. Track complaints, opt-outs and misunderstanding signals as guardrails. Any commercial KPI would require access to internal data and approved experiment design.

## 6. Proposed 90-day roadmap
| Period | Priority | Deliverable | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Days 1–30 | Validate opportunity | Interview plan, baseline disclosure audit, segment sizing request, compliance review | Is the problem real and material? |
| Days 31–60 | Test positioning | Three message territories, prototype, comprehension sessions | Does C improve understanding without harming risk perception? |
| Days 61–90 | Decide on controlled rollout | Approved messaging, channel plan, experiment specification, measurement dashboard | Only proceed with evidence, eligibility and compliance sign-off |

**Dependencies:** internal product analytics, customer research access, actual UK eligibility and fee terms, risk/legal review, and stakeholder approval. No numerical uplift or business impact is claimed.
